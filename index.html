Heritage & Legend

The Literary Spirit of Paris

Founded under the Third Republic around 1887, Café de Flore takes its name from the statue of the goddess of vegetation on Boulevard Saint-Germain. It quickly became the cradle of Paris's greatest intellectual and artistic revolutions.

Legendary figures such as Jean-Paul Sartre, Simone de Beauvoir, Guillaume Apollinaire, Ernest Hemingway, and Pablo Picasso gathered here to reimagine the world over the warmth of a coal stove.

"The roads to freedom passed through the Flore. We were settled there permanently — from nine in the morning until noon, we worked." — Simone de Beauvoir
1887
Founded on Boulevard Saint-Germain
Opened by M. Boubal, it quickly became the meeting place for royalist writers and poets of the era.
1939
The Existentialist Era
Sartre and Simone de Beauvoir made the Flore their permanent office, writing their masterworks here.
1994
Creation of the Prix de Flore
Establishment of the celebrated literary prize rewarding promising young authors in French literature.
Today
Tradition & Art of Living
Faithful preservation of traditional recipes — from old-fashioned hot chocolate to the art of white-aproned table service.
